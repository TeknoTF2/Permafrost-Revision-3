Beta Reader Report

Everything else on this site is the editor talking. This page is the reader talking — the unfiltered experience of going through both books cold, the way your eventual audience will. Where I gasped, where I skimmed, where I argued with you, and the questions I'd ask over coffee.

The Read, As It Happened

Where you had me completely

  • Page one. "The axe bit into the wood with a sound like cracking bone" plus a blind protagonist lying to her best friend about God — I was in before the end of the first scene. For what it's worth, I never once considered DNF-ing either book.
  • The campfire wishes (B1 Ch. 6). I knew exactly what you were doing — loading a gun labeled "feelings" — and it worked anyway. Uthesia's "My wife's" made me put the manuscript down for a minute.
  • The maid (B1 Ch. 11). I gasped out loud. I'd been reading Carina as comic relief with an edge; the punch-through recalibrated the entire book in one paragraph. This is your "Red Wedding moment" — the scene readers will spoiler-tag and argue about. Do not soften it.
  • Muse's scream (B1 Ch. 18). First time I cried. "I gave you my EYES!" The ambiguity of the answer is perfect — I've reread it three times and I still can't decide if Naika answered, and I love that I can't.
  • The knock (B1 Ch. 26). I read this at night and regretted it. The Wendigo build-up across twenty-four chapters of dragging sounds is the most patient horror setup I've seen in an unpublished manuscript.
  • The dig (B2 Ch. 1). "Woke up. Dug. Died." I had to sit with this one. It turned every previous "Cain stands back up" beat into a thousand-year-old habit, and it's the moment I started reading him as a tragedy instead of a tank.
  • The surgery (B2 Ch. 8). The best action scene in the series doesn't have a single attack in it. I was counting bag-squeezes along with her.
  • The dream (B2 Ch. 18). Second cry. The four empty cots. Jae's bird scratched into the frame. "Nobody asked." This is the best chapter you have ever written, and I'd bet money on that without seeing anything else you've written.
  • The reunion (B2 Ch. 19). Third cry — "Are you ok?" as the first words after everything. Then Cain drinking the potion and saying "You saved us." You understand that forgiveness scenes work through actions accepting the offering, not speeches. Most published authors don't.
  • "You probably just annoyed me." (B2 Ch. 39). The most chilling villain line in either book. The genocide didn't even rate a memory. Cain's answering "I just hate you" is its perfect mirror. This duel is the rare revenge climax that refuses to feel good, on purpose.

Where you lost me (temporarily)

  • B1 Ch. 19–20 (the summit). Right after the emotional peak of the entire book, I got fourteen new names, two religions, a trade dispute, and a tent of superheroes. I understand why — the series needs the outside world — but this is where my reading pace dropped for the only time in Book One. Possible fixes: seed one short outside-world scene around Ch. 10 so the summit isn't a cold open, or trim the two chapters into one and a half.
  • B2 Ch. 32–33 (the seminar road / the library maze). Yoo's Murim lecture is interesting content delivered as a syllabus, and the maze resolves by tantrum rather than cleverness. Both chapters are likable; neither is load-bearing at its current length. This is where I checked the remaining page count.
  • POV whiplash in B2's middle. Between chapters 12 and 31 the book cycles Sunforged → Dantalion → Carina/Billy → Powers → Sunforged at irregular intervals. Every individual scene earned its place; the rhythm didn't. When I left the party for two chapters, I started skimming villain pages to get back — which villains this good don't deserve. A regular interlude cadence (every third chapter, say) would have kept me patient.
  • "Where's Orthek?" I wrote this in my notes four separate times during Book Two's crises. He's established as both able and willing, then absent for thirty chapters with no on-page reason. One line — he's beyond the northern river, out of reach, anything — buys total immunity from the question.

Things that confused me (genuinely, not rhetorically)

  • Whether Yoo is dead. "The light left her eyes" reads as death; Carina checking a pulse and fleeing reads as ambiguous; cultivation-world rules make survival plausible. I genuinely don't know what you intend, and unlike the Naika ambiguity (which is productive), this one just left me unsure what I'd read. Decide the reader's exact level of doubt and stage to it.
  • Whether Cain is dead-dead. I assume his Rule revives him — the book taught me that rule in Ch. 1. So his "death" scene read to me as a pause, which may undercut the intended weight. If you want grief, complicate the Rule (a cost, a delay, a question of what wakes up). If you want certainty, show the ember. The current silence gets neither full grief nor full hope.
  • The Wendigo's words in Elizabeth's mouth. "Out of everyone I've wronged, she deserved it the most" — when Elizabeth said it, I flipped back to the cave chapter to check. Verbatim. Is the implication that the Wendigo lifted a real memory from Uthesia's psyche? That Dantalion really said it? I love this echo and I need Book Three to know it happened.
  • The green alkanet's location. The party hauled Barbatos's only counteragent across the country without knowing it, then the ship passes out of the story (Ghisara sent to search it, off-page). After two books of immaculate Chekhov management, this gun is still on the wall at curtain. Intentional, I assume — but it's the thread I'd most want assured isn't forgotten.
  • Minor: where the Gallows Horn summon was during the final Solarspire stretch; whether Muse's Xeroc channel persists alongside Sil'Sot's (the "divine marketplace" rules); and what happened to Dantalion's necrotic charges around Solarspire — armed in Ch. 29, never disarmed on-page. That last one might be a live bomb you've left in the city, which is either an error or Book Three's opening. I can't tell which, and I'd like to.

Things I argued with the book about

  • The ending. Let me be precise, because this is the most important note in this report. Each individual collapse is earned: Cain's hatred, Muse's independence, Uthesia's lone hunt, Gormund's relapse-and-rally, Carina's regression. I believe every one. But stacked in sequence with zero counterweight, the cumulative message a reader receives is not "this is dark" — it's "the author may not intend to give any of this back." That's the difference between tragedy and abandonment. You don't need to soften a single event. You need one ember, deliberately placed, in the final pages: Gormund walking north (he's already your hope-shaped character, and his chapter ends four chapters too early to do this job), frost melting over Cain's chest, the eye of Xeroc opening over Muse — anything that says the story knows the way back. Trust me as your test case: I'm invested, I'm sophisticated about dark fiction, and even I closed Book Two feeling slightly punished for loving these people.
  • Ghisara killing Carina. I went back and forth for a full day. Verdict: keep it — but I want the narrative to eventually charge her for it, even gently. Right now Fafnir scolds her about scroll economics, which is funny but lets the moral question evaporate. Someone — Carina herself, ideally — should one day ask, "What if there'd been no scroll?"
  • Cain leaving the party off a half-finished argument. In character? Yes. But because we never re-enter his POV between the stairwell and the shopping montage, his departure read as colder than I think you intend. One paragraph inside his head would have kept me on his side. (I stayed on Muse's. I suspect you wanted me torn.)
  • Skogsund. "Tastes practically just like pork." I see what you did. I see that Barbatos saw it, tipped, and filed it as leverage. I am begging you to confirm in Book Three that I read that right, because if so it's the most quietly horrifying worldbuilding in the series, and if not, I've slandered an entire fictional town.

Line-level reader experience

  • Your dialogue is the prose's best feature. I could identify all five leads (plus Billy, Dantalion, Elizabeth, Ghisara) from unattributed lines by mid-Book One. That's rare.
  • I noticed the repetitions. By Book Two I was clocking "the kind of X that," "not X, but Y," "something that," and the silence/quiet/voice/looked cluster on a conscious level — which means a copyeditor will too. The Data page has the full audit. This is the single highest-leverage polish item.
  • Interiority volume. The italic-thought convention is great, but in heavy scenes characters sometimes think a feeling the scene has already shown me (Cain's panic spiral, Carina's self-justifications). Cutting one of every three "explainer thoughts" would make the remainder hit harder.
  • Chapter endings are consistently excellent. I kept reading "one more chapter" because of them. Whatever instinct produces your last lines — protect it.

Questions I'd ask you over coffee

  1. Is Yoo alive? (Don't tell me. Tell the manuscript.)
  2. Who is the Merchant's client, and is it the same "contact" who gave Dantalion the artifact? If yes — chef's kiss; seed one more visible link.
  3. What does Naika's "fall" actually mean, given Xeroc's "that's not the word I would use"? This is the series' best mystery; I want assurance it has an answer, not just an aura.
  4. Was the Wendigo's Dantalion line a real memory? (See above. I will not let this go.)
  5. What is Cain's Rule, exactly — and does dragging a soul back have a price you've been saving?
  6. Is Book Three the five separate roads converging, or a new configuration entirely? My reader-heart needs the campfire promises (the drink, the fight, the prayer, the grave, the sun) to still be the series' destination, even if the route is ruins.

Beta Verdict

I would buy Book One today, preorder Book Two, and — with one ember added to its final pages — wait impatiently for Book Three. The found-family material is genuinely top-tier; the dark turn is earned; the craft is visible everywhere. My loyalty as a reader survived everything you did to these characters. Just barely. Which I suspect is exactly the temperature you were aiming for — but know that you are at the line, not safely behind it.
