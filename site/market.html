Genre Positioning

Shelf label: Adult dark epic fantasy / survival fantasy, with progression-fantasy and wuxia-murim crossover appeal. The fusion is the product: Western D&D-flavored epic fantasy (paladins, clerics, dungeon breaks, adventurers' guild) × Korean murim/cultivation (sects, ki, the Heavenly Demon) × industrial frontier tech (gunslingers, golems, ironclads). No major traditionally-published series currently occupies this exact intersection, while the appetite for it is proven at enormous scale on the serial platforms (Royal Road, Webnovel) and in translated webnovels/manhwa. That's the commercial thesis: manhwa-native sensibilities delivered with trade-fantasy prose craft.

The Hooks (use these everywhere)

  1. The premise hook: A magical dome seals an entire country in endless winter. Outside, the world's powers argue. Inside, five strangers walk toward the answer.
  2. The character hook: A blind prophet keeps her dead goddess "alive" by lying to the last man who believes — and he'd rather die than learn the truth.
  3. The dark hook: The party's youngest member ends a hostage crisis by punching through the hostage. The book is about everything that costs.
  4. The scene hook (sample-pages strategy): the blind surgery; the knock at the cabin door; "Open wide." Any one of these as an excerpt sells the craft instantly.
  5. The series hook: Book One builds the family. Book Two breaks it. Book Three has to decide what survives the thaw.

Draft Back-Cover Copy (Book One)

The sky broke first. Then came the cold.

A dome of ice has sealed the kingdom of Kordova away from the sun, and every day the frost creeps deeper. In the dying village of Velldale, five strangers take a desperate contract: walk west, find the capital, find answers.

A blind prophet whose goddess has gone silent. An ancient paladin who cannot die — and cannot forget the man who burned his faith. A dwarf who can fix anything except himself. A widow with her dead bound in her veins. And a nineteen-year-old martial prodigy raised to be a weapon, who is about to show them exactly what that means.

The cold is patient. It finds whatever you use to keep warm — faith, family, honor, love — and it presses until something cracks.

Two hundred and six people are counting on them. The first to fall won't be killed by the ice.

Audience Profile

  • Core: Adult readers 18–45 of dark/grimdark epic fantasy (Abercrombie, Lawrence, Cook readers) who also consume manhwa/anime — a demographic that is huge and chronically under-served by trade publishing.
  • Crossover: Progression/LitRPG-adjacent serial readers (guild ranks, power tiers, named techniques scratch the itch without stat screens); horror-tolerant fantasy readers (the Wendigo arc is a genuine selling point); character-first readers who made Sword of Kaigen and Six of Crows word-of-mouth hits.
  • Notable asset: Muse is meaningful blind representation executed with craft (sensory-first POV, no magical cure). This earns reviewer attention and library/list placement — and it's authentic to the text, not a marketing veneer.
  • Content advisories to plan for: graphic violence including a hostage's death, child-soldier backstory and the culling of children, grooming dynamics (Billy/Carina — written critically, but it will be discussed), self-destructive sexuality at the B2 ending, animal death, addiction/relapse, implied cannibalism, parental death. None of this is gratuitous; all of it belongs on a CW list for serial platforms.

Path Analysis

Traditional

  • Book One queries cleanly at 95k. Lead with Muse + the dome premise; sample pages = Ch. 1 (the lie lands on page 2 — agents will see the hook immediately).
  • The risk in this channel is the Book Two ending: acquiring editors will want assurance the series isn't nihilism without an exit. The fix (one embedded ember, plus a one-page series arc in the synopsis showing the thaw) is cheap and decisive.
  • The murim element is a differentiator now in trade publishing — the manhwa wave is cresting into print. Timing favors you.

Indie / Serial

  • The chapter craft (77% of chapters end on hooks; 3–4k lengths) is already serial-optimized. Royal Road → Kindle Unlimited is a proven pipeline for exactly this fusion.
  • Serial readers will tolerate — even celebrate — the dark B2 ending if Book Three chapters start landing on schedule. The danger zone is a long gap after Ch. 40.
  • Inter-book structure suggestion for serial release: the B1 Ch. 19–20 outside-world chapters perform better in serial (readers vote on arcs) than in a single sitting — no change needed for this path.

Series Strategy Notes

  • The promise ledger is your marketing spine. Five wishes were made at a campfire: a drink, a fight, a prayer, a grave, the sun. Book Three's marketing (and its ending) should be built on that list — it's already the thing beta readers cite unprompted.
  • Track the open threads publicly cited on this site (dome source, Naika's fall, green alkanet, the Merchant's client, Everos, Yoo's fate, Cain's Rule, Skogsund, the necrotic charges). A visible series bible protects you; nine-plus active mysteries is an asset only while readers trust the ledger.
  • Title/branding: "Permafrost" is strong — evocative, one word, on-theme (permanent cold; what's preserved beneath; what thaws). Consider subtitle differentiation per volume (e.g., Permafrost: The Long Dark / The Thaw) for shelf clarity.
  • Adaptation surface: The property is unusually animation-ready (party-of-five, episodic settlements, signature fight choreography, manhwa-native power aesthetics). The blind-surgery and knock scenes are storyboard-ready set pieces. Worth one line in pitch materials.
