Book Two — Chapter by Chapter

All forty chapters. Average rating: 8.5 — higher peaks than Book One, slightly lower floor in the mid-book political stretch.

The dig

Chapter 1 9.5

Does well The most audacious backstory chapter in the series: Cain's thousand years rendered as a loop — die, wake, dig, die — dragging Muse's coffin upward through the dark. The faith-vs-Fey-Rule ambiguity ("Cain didn't care. He needed the meaning.") is theology and characterization fused. Bookending with the present (Uthesia catching him at the door; "You're the wall, I'm the blade") converts revelation into momentum.

Could improve The Fey Rule paragraph repeats its "anyone who knew anything about fey would argue" framing twice nearly verbatim — once is a wink, twice is a seam.

How it felt Horrified awe. "He found the coffin" might be the most romantic sentence in the series, in the old sense of the word.

Yoo · The Tablet

Chapter 2 7.5

Does well Drachenburg erased under snow is a chilling establishing image, and the Tablet meditation embeds the world's power rankings in personal grief — Yoo's eyes stopping at rank 3, the un-nameable thought about her daughter, is elegant withholding. Solarspire's civil war from a tower-top is efficient and grim. Fafnir's deal gives the outside plot its spine.

Could improve The Guild-history lecture (Age of Corpses → ranks → God titles) runs three pages essentially unbroken; half could surface later through use rather than recital.

How it felt Informative — the chapter where I took notes rather than felt things. Worth it for "Give me an hour."
