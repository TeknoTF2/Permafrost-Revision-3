Sunforged — The Five

Muse

Blind Prophet · The Lying Saint

A prophet whose goddess is gone, lying to the last believer to protect his faith. The emotional center of the series and its most original creation.

Cain

Satyr Paladin · The Wall That Cracks

A thousand-year-old soldier who cannot die and cannot forgive. His vow against killing humans is the series' longest fuse — and Book Two lights it.

Carina

Murim Prodigy · The Weapon Who Wants a Family

Nineteen, unbeatable, unbearable, and broken in ways she has no vocabulary for. The series' most ambitious arc and its most devastating ending.

Gormund

Dwarf Craftsman · The One Who Stands Back Up

Refugee, field medic, dragon-slayer-by-hammer-throw, functional alcoholic. The most grounded arc in the series and its quiet moral backbone.

Uthesia

Eladrin Soul-Binder · The Locked Chest

A widow carrying her dead team in her own veins, hunting the Lord who killed them. Revenge given two books of patience — then a cell.

The Orbit

Yoo Yeon

Lightning of Mount Hua · The Mother Who Chose Too Late

One of the strongest people alive, undone by the one fight she refused to have. Her confession and final duel are Book Two's emotional summit.

Billy Kidd

Outlaw Satyr · The Best-Tasting Poison

The most dangerous character in the series isn't a Lord — it's a charming gunslinger who tells lonely girls they were right all along.

Barbatos

Lord of Poison · Atrocity as Philanthropy

Rank eight of the 72, destroyer of the Solar Covenant, professional humanitarian. He forgot the genocide. That's the point.

Dantalion / Everos

Lord of Knowledge · The Man Who Planned His Own Murder

The dome's accidental architect, a doting father, a remote-control warlord — and Book Three's lich-shaped problem.

Elizabeth

Heir of Knowledge · Fourteen Going on Tyrant

A child prodigy who kills kings, frames Lords, and out-lectures her father. The series' most unsettling creation.

Orthek

Dragon God · The Ceiling of the World

Rank one of the Ten Great Powers, a retired god raising a daughter — and the moral argument for restraint, embodied.
